## How to read these numbers (added 2026-11-16, no rerun)

- **k and N.** k = 5 over N = 9 retrievable documents: the corpus has 10 documents (10 chunks, one chunk
  each), and the one personal document is outside the public scope. k/N = 5/9 = 0.56.
- **Random baseline.** A ranker that orders the 9 documents at random puts 5/9 = 0.56 of any expected
  document in the top 5. Averaged over the 11 cases with expected documents (case 10's `host_note` filter
  leaves a single document, so its baseline is 1.00) the **random baseline is recall@5 = 0.60 and
  MRR = 0.40**. Every arm is far above it, but the distance between arms is not.
- **Measured vs demonstrated.** The latency columns are measurements (every case, every arm). The ranking
  columns are not a measurement of which strategy ranks better: keyword 1.00 vs vector 0.95 is **one
  case** (06, where vector found 1 of 2 documents), and the MRR differences come from **cases 03 and 04**.
  They demonstrate a mechanism (vectors rank a paraphrase target first where keyword ranks it second;
  keyword finds an exact term that vector misses) on one or two cases; a corpus of this size cannot rank
  the strategies.
