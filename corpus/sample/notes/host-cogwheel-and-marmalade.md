# Host notes: Cogwheel & Marmalade Ltd. (fictional)

What we learned hosting the autumn summit in the Kettle Room, written up so the next organiser does
not have to rediscover it. Cogwheel & Marmalade is a fictional workshop-tools company with a large
ground-floor event space, a friendly facilities team and some firm rules. Our contact there is the
facilities desk, not an individual, and every request goes through the shared facilities inbox at
least a week ahead. They are happy to host again in spring if we keep to the rules below, which
mostly come down to planning ahead and leaving the room as we found it. The notes are grouped by the
part of the day they matter for.

## Arrival and security

Reception needs the final attendee count, not the names, five working days before the event; they
print numbered visitor badges and hand them out at the door. Speakers and volunteers get separate
badges, so send their count separately. Deliveries and equipment come in through the freight lift at
the back of the building, which only runs until 09:00 on weekends, so anything heavy has to arrive
early. Volunteers should carry the folding signs from the bus stop to the side entrance before the
doors open. Nobody may prop open the fire doors, not even for a few minutes while unloading.

## Parking and transport

There is no visitor parking on site; the car park belongs to the fictional tenants upstairs and is
patrolled. Point attendees to the tram stop two streets away and the bus stop on Bramble Lane, and put
both in the registration confirmation. Taxis and ride shares can stop at the side entrance for a few
minutes only. Speakers who bring equipment can unload at the freight lift before 09:00 and then park
in the public garage on the next street, which the host does not pay for. Bikes go in the covered
racks behind the building, not in the atrium.

## Audio-visual

The Kettle Room projector only takes DisplayPort; there is no HDMI input at all, so bring
DisplayPort adapters for every common laptop. Two wireless handheld microphones and one lapel
microphone are available; the batteries are charged by the facilities team the evening before. The
room has a hearing loop, which has to be switched on at the AV cabinet by the door. Recording is
allowed, but the host's own camera rig is not available on weekends, so bring a camera and a tripod.
The venue Wi-Fi password changes every day and is printed on the badge, so speakers should not rely on
having it in advance.

## Food and drink

Catering comes from the host's approved caterer, the fictional Saffron Spoon kitchen; outside food is
not allowed, so pizza deliveries are out. The catering order, with numbers and dietary needs, must be
confirmed seventy-two hours before the event, and the numbers cannot go down after that. Food and
drink stay in the atrium: nothing to eat or drink in the Kettle Room except water in closed bottles.
Allergen labels are printed by the caterer. Coffee urns are refilled once in the afternoon, at 15:00.

## After the event

Everyone has to be out of the building by 18:00; the night porter locks the side entrance at 18:15.
Chairs go back into the stacks along the north wall, and recycling is sorted into the three bins in
the atrium. The keys to the AV cabinet go back to the night porter, never to reception. Lost property
is kept for a week. The facilities team asks for a short thank-you note afterwards; it helps when we
ask to come back.
